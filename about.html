Field Notes

These compiled field notes are condensed summaries of eyewitness reports, S.T.A.R.S. debriefs, and leaked field logs from early containment teams. They trace the first anomalous incidents in the Arklay Mountains, the Raccoon City cluster, and subsequent global flare‑ups. The earliest entries describe aggressive behavioral changes in infected hosts, accelerated tissue necrosis, and unexpected neurotropic symptoms that suggested a viral agent engineered to alter host cognition.

Key eyewitness fragments

  • Arklay Research Outpost — Night patrols reported "dog‑like" creatures with coordinated hunting behavior; initial assumption: mutated canids from contaminated feed.
  • S.T.A.R.S. Bravo Team — Reconnaissance footage shows rapid decomposition and sporadic regeneration in soft tissue; several team members required emergency extraction.
  • Raccoon City Civilian Reports — Early civilian calls referenced "flu‑like" symptoms followed by violent delirium and loss of higher reasoning within 24–48 hours.

Note: these notes synthesize canonical events from Resident Evil series sources into a single narrative for UX storytelling; they are summaries, not verbatim transcripts.

Corporate Dossier

Umbrella Corporation began as a multinational pharmaceutical conglomerate with legitimate public projects. Behind the public facade, Umbrella funded clandestine bioengineering programs: the T‑Virus (a mutagenic viral agent), the G‑Virus (a recombinant virus with rapid cellular regeneration properties), and numerous derivative projects. Canon sources identify key figures and events: Oswell E. Spencer (founder and patron of genetic research), William Birkin (G‑Virus developer), and Albert Wesker (S.T.A.R.S. captain and covert Umbrella operative).

Corporate structure and coverups

  • Shell companies and subsidiaries — Umbrella used layers of corporate entities to fund off‑books research and to distance the parent company from field operations.
  • Paramilitary retrieval — Umbrella contracted private security and field teams to recover test subjects and suppress leaks; these teams often escalated incidents.
  • Legal suppression — Settlements, gag orders, and targeted disinformation campaigns were used to erase public records and intimidate whistleblowers.

The dossier also traces the arc of Umbrella's downfall: internal betrayals, the exposure of the G‑Virus, and the eventual public collapse after Raccoon City's destruction. Post‑Umbrella, successor organizations and rogue scientists continued to weaponize viral research, creating new threats and splinter outbreaks.

Global Outbreak Timeline — Canon Summary

Pre‑1998 — Early Research and Arklay

Umbrella's Arklay research outpost conducted animal trials and early viral experiments. The Spencer Mansion incident exposed S.T.A.R.S. to the T‑Virus and related bio‑weapons research, setting off the first documented field encounters with reanimated hosts.

1998 — Raccoon City and the G‑Virus

William Birkin's G‑Virus research in Raccoon City produced a highly unstable but potent pathogen. After Birkin's attempt to protect his research, the virus spread through Umbrella's urban labs and into the city, leading to mass infection. The city was ultimately incinerated in an attempt to contain the outbreak.

Post‑Raccoon — Umbrella Collapse and Legacy

Umbrella's public exposure led to legal action and corporate collapse, but the technology and personnel dispersed. Rogue scientists, black‑market bioengineering, and militarized viral programs continued to produce localized outbreaks and new viral strains in later years.

Umbrella Corps Dossier — Canon Profiles

Oswell E. Spencer

One of Umbrella's founders, Spencer championed genetic research and the pursuit of human evolution through biotechnology. His patronage funded secret projects and private facilities that operated beyond public oversight.

William Birkin

A lead researcher on the G‑Virus project, Birkin's work produced a virus capable of rapid cellular regeneration and extreme mutation. His refusal to surrender the research and the subsequent skirmish in Raccoon City were pivotal in the city's downfall.

Albert Wesker

Initially a S.T.A.R.S. team leader, Wesker was later revealed to be a double agent with ties to Umbrella. His manipulation of field events and pursuit of superhuman abilities make him one of the franchise's central antagonists.

Ethics and Aftermath

Umbrella's ethical violations included human experimentation, unauthorized field tests, and deliberate misinformation. After Umbrella's collapse, the knowledge and personnel fragmented into private contractors, black‑market labs, and militarized programs—ensuring the threat persisted in new forms.
